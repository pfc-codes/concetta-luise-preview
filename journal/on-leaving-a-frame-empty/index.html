On leaving a frame empty

A short sample journal entry about attention, absence and the space around an image.

An empty frame changes the room. Before there is an image to interpret, there is a boundary to notice: a decision about where looking might begin.

A space held open

In an archive, absence can be more useful than an approximation. A missing photograph need not be replaced by a convenient image. It can remain a space held open for the right record.

The frame makes that decision visible. It offers a pause in the sequence of names, places and dates. It lets the reader imagine the encounter without pretending to document it.

A frame is also a promise to return.
